## FAQ

**What are institutional accumulation stocks?**

Institutional accumulation stocks are companies where large funds, such as pension funds, hedge funds, or endowments, are actively building positions. These investors trade millions of shares per order, far exceeding retail volumes. Their buying shows up as rising volume and steady price support, often before a major breakout. Tracking this accumulation can signal upcoming strength.

**How can I identify institutional accumulation in a stock?**

Use volume-based indicators like On-Balance Volume (OBV) or the Accumulation/Distribution line to spot consistent buying pressure. Also review 13F filings, which list institutional holdings quarterly. A steady increase in institutional ownership, combined with rising volume on up days, confirms accumulation. The price may stay flat while volume builds, which is the early signal.

**What is a valid breakout pattern in trading?**

A valid breakout requires two conditions: volume surges at least 50% above the average daily volume, and the price closes in the upper half of the daily range. Without volume, a price move is weak and likely to fail. The closing range confirms that buyers held control through the session. Both conditions together reduce the risk of false breakouts.

**How does volume confirmation help in breakout trading?**

Volume confirmation ensures that a breakout has real participation, not just a thin price move. When volume surges 50% above average, it signals that institutions are involved. This reduces the chance of a false breakout, which often occurs on low volume. High volume breakouts hold better, providing a more reliable entry point.
