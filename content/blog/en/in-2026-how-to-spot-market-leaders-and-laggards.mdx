## FAQ

**What is leading the market in 2026?**

S&P 600 Value leads with a 10.9% YTD return, outpacing every major cap and style group. Mid-caps follow closely, with all S&P 400 styles near 8.8%. The S&P 500 trails at 1.8%, reflecting a sharp rotation away from large-cap growth leadership toward smaller companies and value factors.

**Why is the S&P 500 underperforming smaller indices?**

The S&P 500's 1.8% YTD gain lags because its largest constituents carried heavy 2025 multiples. When growth expectations reset, mega-cap tech drag offsets strength elsewhere. The Nasdaq Composite dropping 0.83% on August 4 confirms this pressure. 2026 rewards breadth, not concentration in a few names.

**How does AI infrastructure spending affect sector rotation?**

AI infrastructure spending lifts industrials through capital investment in electricity and defense. But it punishes companies bearing the cost. SpaceX dropped 13% after reporting $18.4 billion in Q2 capital expenditures. Nvidia rose 3% on the SpaceX processor deal. The spending benefits chipmakers while pressuring capital-intensive operators.

**What risks face the Energy sector after its earnings jump?**

Energy sector earnings jumped on higher oil prices, but elevated expectations now pose the primary risk. Valuations have risen to reflect those earnings, leaving little room for disappointment. If oil prices stabilize or decline, the sector faces compression from both directions. Schwab flags this valuation risk clearly.

**Why did AMD fall 7% despite meeting earnings expectations?**

AMD fell 7% after posting adjusted earnings that only matched consensus. In a market reassessing tech multiples, meeting expectations is no longer enough. The decline signals zero tolerance for in-line results from growth names. This aligns with broader Nasdaq weakness and the rotation away from 2025's growth leadership.

**Should investors shift from large-cap growth to small-cap value?**

S&P 600 Value's 10.9% YTD lead over the S&P 500's 1.8% argues for reducing mega-cap concentration. But rotation can reverse quickly if credit tightens or AI spending slows. Mid-caps at 8.8% offer a middle path. The data supports tilting toward breadth, not abandoning large-caps entirely.
