The win rate spans trades reported over multiple years, not a single consistent holding period.

## The four-day delay problem

![Close-up of calculator, pen, and magnifying glass on financial documents.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/13156204/pexels-photo-13156204.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&h=650&w=940)

STOCK Act disclosures are not filed on the trade date; representatives and senators face a 45-day filing window.

Recent CongressFlow records show a typical delay: Representative April Delaney sold STE on July 31, 2026, but the filing appeared August 4, 2026, a four-day gap, per [CongressFlow](https://congressflow.com/trades).

The same CongressFlow record shows Delaney bought FWONK on July 31, 2026, filed August 4, 2026, with a $15,001 to $50,000 range.

Disclosure ranges, not exact prices, mean copy traders only see a bracket like $1,001 to $15,000, per [CongressFlow](https://congressflow.com/trades).

A four-day delay in a fast-moving stock can erase the entire edge the original trade captured.

Copy traders pay bid-ask spreads and commissions that Pelosi does not, further eroding returns.

Pelosi herself gains no direct financial benefit from the trackers, since her trades are public filings.

The true cost of copying is hidden: the delay, the spread, and the tax bill on short-term gains.

## The ethics question nobody answers

The STOCK Act of 2012 requires members of Congress to disclose trades within 45 days, but it does not ban trading.

Critics argue that members can still trade on non-public information gleaned from committee briefings and hearings.

Pelosi has faced repeated questions about whether her trading advantages come from her position, though no formal accusation is in the research dump.

Trackers highlight the returns but rarely mention the ethical controversy surrounding congressional stock trading.

The debate has fueled calls for a ban on individual stock trading by members of Congress, though no such ban passed as of the data shown.

## FAQ

**What is Nancy Pelosi's current stock portfolio?**

Her exact current portfolio is not publicly listed in a single document. The most recent trades appear in her periodic transaction reports, filed with the House Clerk. Trackers like QuiverQuant and InsiderFinance show her reported trades, but the holdings change with each filing. To see the latest, check the House disclosure portal or the tracker sites.

**How can I copy Nancy Pelosi's stock trades legally?**

You can legally copy her trades by using a tracker platform like pelosiTracker.app, which sends alerts when she files a disclosure. The platform then lets you place the same trade through a connected broker. The key limit is the delay: you only see the trade after the filing, not the same day. Still, it is fully legal because the data comes from public filings.

**What is the current win rate on Nancy Pelosi's reported trades?**

InsiderFinance reports an 88.4% win rate on her tracked trades, based on 61 wins and 6 losses. That number covers 212 reported trades with a total volume of $268.5 million. The win rate only counts closed positions, so it can shift as new filings come in. It also does not reflect the size of each gain or loss.

**Is it legal for members of Congress to trade stocks?**

Yes, it is legal, with limits. The STOCK Act of 2012 requires members to disclose trades within 45 days, and they cannot use non-public information. They must also report trades over $1,000. There is no blanket ban on trading, though some lawmakers have proposed one. The rules apply to senators, representatives, and their immediate families.

**How do Pelosi portfolio trackers get their data?**

Trackers pull data from the official House and Senate disclosure portals. The House Clerk publishes financial disclosures, and the Senate eFD portal requires users to agree to restrictions before viewing. The trackers scrape these filings, parse the trade details, and display them in a dashboard. They also calculate performance metrics like win rate and net worth estimates.

**What are the best trackers for Congress stock trades?**

Popular options include QuiverQuant, which shows trading activity by politician, and pelosiTracker.app, which offers copy-trading features. InsiderFinance provides net worth and win-rate data. For raw filings, the House Clerk and Senate eFD portals are the primary sources. Each tracker has different features, so compare the data freshness and the cost before choosing one.
