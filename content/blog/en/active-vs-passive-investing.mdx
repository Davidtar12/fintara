Active vs passive investing compares two approaches to portfolio management. Active investing involves picking stocks or other assets to outperform a benchmark, often through research and market timing, while passive investing seeks to match market returns by tracking an index at low cost. This debate matters because fees, performance, and investor behavior have significant long-term impact on wealth accumulation; the choice affects returns, volatility, and complexity for individual and institutional investors.

## What the data shows

- S&P SPIVA U.S. Scorecard (2023): 89% of large-cap active funds underperformed their benchmark over 10 years
- Average expense ratio for passive index funds: 0.04% (Vanguard S&P 500 ETF); average active fund: 0.66% (ICI data)
- Vanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund (VTSAX) has a 0.04% expense ratio
